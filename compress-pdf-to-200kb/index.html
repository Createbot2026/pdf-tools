Compress a PDF to under 200KB

200KB is one of the tightest limits you will meet, common on older recruitment systems, exam boards and council forms. A one-page scanned letter usually fits; a 10-page scan often will not without dropping to greyscale.

    How to do it

    1. Choose your PDF (or drag it onto the box).
    2. Leave the target at 200 KB, or tick greyscale for scans of black-and-white documents.
    3. Press Compress. The tool tries progressively lower image quality until the file is under 200KB.
    4. Download the result and open it to check the text is still readable before you submit it.

    Tips and limits

    • Text-only PDFs exported from Word are usually already small; if yours is over 200KB it is normally because of embedded images or fonts.
    • For scans, greyscale roughly halves the size with almost no readability loss.
    • If you cannot reach 200KB, split the document and upload the essential pages only, if the form allows it.
    • Compression here turns pages into images, so the text in the output is not selectable. Keep your original.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does my PDF stay above 200KB?

    Each page becomes a compressed image. Very long documents (roughly 8+ pages) can exceed 200KB even at low quality. Remove non-essential pages with the extract tool first.

    Will the PDF still be readable?

    At 200KB a one to three page document stays clearly readable. Zoom in on the downloaded file to check small print before submitting.

    Is my file uploaded anywhere?

    No. The tool runs in your browser with JavaScript. The PDF is read from your device, processed in memory and saved back to your device. You can switch off Wi-Fi after the page loads and it still works.
